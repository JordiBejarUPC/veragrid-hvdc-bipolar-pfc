SMALL-SIGNAL STABILITY BENCHMARK: ANDES VS VERAGRID
==================================================

Summary date: 2026-08-12
Analyzed cases: Kundur, IEEE14, IEEE39, NPCC140, and WECC179.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------

All five cases show very good numerical agreement between ANDES and VeraGrid.
All available states and eigenvalues have been included in the comparison. The
observed differences are small enough to conclude that VeraGrid correctly
reproduces the ANDES small-signal results for these benchmarks.

The best overall agreement is obtained for IEEE14 and NPCC140. WECC179 has the
largest absolute differences, but it is also the largest system and contains
state-matrix entries of up to approximately 540,000. Its relative state-matrix
error is still only 6.02e-7.

Regarding computation time, ANDES is between 2.94 and 5.70 times faster in the
end-to-end execution time measured in these runs. VeraGrid's main bottleneck is
the symbolic-to-numeric conversion, particularly for NPCC140 and WECC179. In
contrast, VeraGrid is faster in the isolated eigenvalue and participation-factor
calculation for every case.

IEEE14

WECC179

- Mean absolute eigenvalue error: 4.376489e-6 rad/s.
- Relative state-matrix error: 6.023567e-7.

- Relative participation-factor error: 3.075126e-5.

- Result: good agreement for the largest and most demanding benchmark.

Timing interpretation:

- ANDES is faster in symbolic-to-numeric conversion and isolated state-matrix
  construction.
- The symbolic-conversion difference grows significantly for NPCC140 and
  WECC179 and is the main bottleneck identified in VeraGrid.
- VeraGrid is faster in the isolated eigenvalue and participation-factor
  calculation for all five cases.
- For the complete small-signal phase, VeraGrid is faster for IEEE39 and
  NPCC140, while ANDES is faster for Kundur, IEEE14, and WECC179.
- The end-to-end time includes case construction, power flow, numerical
  preparation, small-signal analysis, and result export. The isolated diagnostic
  repeats are not added to the total time again.
- ANDES has its own dynamic-initialization and benchmark-preprocessing phases
  without an identified one-to-one phase in VeraGrid. These entries are shown as
  unavailable in the timing comparison table.

Measured timing phases:

1. case_and_model_build: case loading and model construction.
2. power_flow: power-flow solution.
3. symbolic_to_numeric: conversion of symbolic equations to numerical
   functions and matrices.
4. dynamic_initialization: ANDES dynamic initialization.
5. benchmark_preprocessing: benchmark-specific preparation.
6. small_signal_total: complete small-signal calculation.
7. state_matrix_isolated_repeat: isolated state-matrix construction.
8. eigenanalysis_isolated_repeat: eigenvalues, eigenvectors, and participation
   factors.
9. result_export: CSV, JSON, report, and figure generation.
10. end_to_end: complete execution time, excluding the isolated diagnostic
    repeats from being counted a second time.

4. CONCLUSION
-------------

The numerical validation is satisfactory for all five systems. VeraGrid and
ANDES produce equivalent operating points, state matrices, eigenvalues, and
participation factors within very small numerical errors. No significant
physical discrepancy has been identified in the final results.

The main remaining difference is performance. VeraGrid is already particularly
competitive in modal analysis, but symbolic-to-numeric conversion and
state-matrix construction penalize the total execution time, especially as the
system size increases.

A definitive statistical performance study should repeat each case several
times, discard the first execution, and compare the median, mean, and dispersion.
The current measurements are suitable for a quick comparison and for identifying
the main bottlenecks.
