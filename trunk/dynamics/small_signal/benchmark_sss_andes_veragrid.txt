SMALL-SIGNAL STABILITY BENCHMARK: ANDES VS VERAGRID
==================================================

Summary date: 2026-08-13
Analyzed cases: Kundur, IEEE14, IEEE39, NPCC140, and WECC179.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------

All five cases show very close numerical agreement between ANDES and VeraGrid.
Every exported state and eigenvalue is included in the comparison. WECC179 has
21 repeated modes; these modes remain in the eigenvalue comparison and s-domain
plot, but they are excluded from participation-factor error norms because the
eigenvector basis of a repeated eigenspace is not unique.

ANDES completed the authoritative normal workflow between 2.05 and 5.02 times
faster in these single measured runs. This does not mean that every ANDES
numerical kernel is faster. The total difference is mostly caused by power-flow
preparation and RMS-problem construction/compilation. Once the numerical RMS
problem is ready, VeraGrid performs the production small-signal calculation
faster for IEEE39, NPCC140, and WECC179.

VeraGrid DOES perform dynamic initialization. It happens inside construction of
RmsProblemDae and is included in the combined phase named
rms_problem_build_initialization_symbolic_to_numeric. It must not be interpreted
as missing or as having zero cost.

The isolated state-matrix and eigenanalysis measurements are warm diagnostic
repetitions made after the production small-signal run. They are useful for
profiling only and are explicitly EXCLUDED from normal_workflow_total.

3. TIMING DEFINITIONS AND ACCOUNTING
------------------------------------

All values are wall-clock seconds from one execution and were measured with
time.perf_counter.

The relation labels have the following exact meaning:

- IN TOTAL: a mutually exclusive component of the normal workflow.
- TOTAL: the authoritative normal workflow total. It is the elapsed time from
  network creation/loading through export of the numerical result artifacts,
  less the two diagnostic warm repetitions.
- EXCLUDED: a warm repetition executed only to expose a numerical kernel. It is
  not added to the normal workflow total.

small_signal_total is an IN TOTAL production phase. It already performs the
state-matrix construction, eigensolution, participation-factor calculation, and
result assembly once. The two EXCLUDED diagnostic rows repeat matrix
construction and eigenanalysis after that production calculation.

The total excludes writing the timing CSV/JSON themselves, plotting, and the
later ANDES-vs-VeraGrid comparator. result_export includes the engine numerical
result files written for later inspection and comparison.

The column VG/ANDES used below is defined as:

    VG/ANDES = VeraGrid seconds / ANDES seconds

A value above 1 means ANDES was faster. A value below 1 means VeraGrid was
faster. Because these are single-run measurements, factors based on phases below
a few milliseconds must be treated as approximate.

5. QUICK PHASE SCOPE REFERENCE
------------------------------

case_and_model_build
    Load or construct buses, branches, injections, parameters, and dynamic
    model instances. File parsing and model-object creation are included.

andes_system_setup
    ANDES-only setup of arrays, per-unit coefficients, DAE addresses, sparse
    patterns, references, connectivity, and model bookkeeping.

power_flow
    Obtain the steady-state operating point. In VeraGrid this includes numerical
    circuit compilation inside PowerFlowDriver.run(), not only Newton iterations.

dynamic_initialization
    ANDES TDS initialization and first iterative step. VeraGrid performs the
    corresponding dynamic initialization inside its combined RMS-problem phase.

benchmark_preprocessing
    ANDES-only folding of exact zero-time-constant and unused auxiliary states
    required to retain the same physical state inventory as VeraGrid.

rms_problem_build_initialization_symbolic_to_numeric
    VeraGrid-only combined phase: assemble the global RMS DAE, seed and solve
    explicit device initialization, and compile RHS plus the fx, fy, gx, and gy
    Jacobian functions.

small_signal_total
    Production calculation of the state/generalized matrix, all eigenvalues,
    left and right eigenvectors, participation factors, damping/frequency data,
    and result-object assembly.

result_export
    Serialize operating-point, state-matrix, modal, participation-factor, and
    label data. This is an I/O phase, not a numerical solver kernel.

normal_workflow_unattributed_overhead
    Short Python-side gaps between explicit timers. It is retained so all IN
    TOTAL rows reconcile exactly with TOTAL.

diagnostic_state_matrix_warm_repeat
    EXCLUDED warm repeat used to inspect state-matrix construction separately.

diagnostic_eigenanalysis_pf_warm_repeat
    EXCLUDED warm repeat used to inspect eigensolution and participation factors
    separately.

6. PHASE-BY-PHASE TIME COMPARISON
--------------------------------

6.1 Case and model construction
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Case      ANDES [s]  VeraGrid [s]  VG/ANDES  Faster engine
--------  ---------  ------------  --------  -------------
Kundur     0.549292      0.013182    0.024x  VeraGrid 41.7x
IEEE14     0.532739      0.197621    0.371x  VeraGrid 2.70x
IEEE39     0.530226      0.084613    0.160x  VeraGrid 6.27x
NPCC140    1.438050      0.425719    0.296x  VeraGrid 3.38x
WECC179    0.683362      0.576285    0.843x  VeraGrid 1.19x

What happens in this phase:

- ANDES parses JSON/XLSX case data and creates its model, parameter, and service
  objects. Loading available generated numerical calls is normally paid while
  the case is loaded.
- VeraGrid either constructs the benchmark directly or imports a base network
  and assigns RMS templates to every physical device.

Interpretation and scalability:

- VeraGrid is faster in every case, but this is not a pure mathematical scaling
  comparison because the source formats and construction paths differ.
- Kundur is dominated by a fixed ANDES load cost: this phase is 84.8% of its
  complete ANDES workflow despite having only eight states.
- File parsing, disk cache, antivirus, the number of physical devices, and the
  model mix matter more than the number of differential states alone.
- The engines become much closer for WECC179, where model construction is only
  1.19x faster in VeraGrid.

6.2 Power flow
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Case      ANDES [s]  VeraGrid [s]  VG/ANDES
--------  ---------  ------------  --------
Kundur     0.007088      1.690279   238.46x
IEEE14     0.004411      2.014003   456.64x
IEEE39     0.006625      2.235054   337.35x
NPCC140    0.027565      2.114821    76.72x
WECC179    0.006915      2.005538   290.04x

What happens in this phase:

- Both engines solve the steady-state operating point.
- VeraGrid's timed PowerFlowDriver.run() also compiles the MultiCircuit into a
  numerical circuit, builds indices/admittance structures, separates islands,
  solves Newton, and assembles results.
- In ANDES, much of the equivalent structural preparation has already been paid
  in case_and_model_build and andes_system_setup before PFlow.run() is timed.

Interpretation and scalability:

- The 77x--457x factors must NOT be read as saying that VeraGrid's Newton solver
  itself is hundreds of times slower. The two phase boundaries are different.
- VeraGrid stays close to two seconds for all five systems. This indicates a
  dominant fixed or cold preparation cost in the measured path; it is not a
  clear size-dependent power-flow trend.
- Power flow accounts for 92.4%, 65.3%, 65.0%, 40.6%, and 32.4% of the VeraGrid
  total for Kundur through WECC179 respectively. Its percentage falls because
  RMS compilation and export grow, not because the phase becomes faster.
- Solver tolerances also differ by benchmark according to the strictest
  convergent settings, so a pure Newton comparison would require separate
  timers for numerical-circuit compilation, matrix construction, iterations,
  and result processing.

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

VeraGrid reports its combined RMS construction, initialization, and compilation:

--------  ------------------  ---------------------  -------------------
Kundur              0.048181               0.077351                1.61x

NPCC140             0.103285               2.235265               21.64x
WECC179             0.100983               2.598982               25.74x

What happens in these phases:

- ANDES setup prepares DAE arrays and sparse structures. TDS.init() and
  TDS.itm_step() initialize the dynamic operating point. Benchmark preprocessing
  folds zero-mass auxiliary states.
- VeraGrid constructs RmsProblemDae, collects all state/algebraic/differential
  variables, connects every device to its buses, forms nodal equations,
  performs explicit device initialization, and compiles numerical RHS and four
  Jacobian functions.

Interpretation and scalability:

- The displayed ratio is indicative, not strictly like-for-like. ANDES can load
  pregenerated numerical calls during case construction, while VeraGrid pays
  system-specific symbolic compilation in its combined phase.
- VeraGrid grows from 0.077 s for 8 states to 2.599 s for 507 states. The
  observed cost per state falls from approximately 9.7 ms to 5.1 ms, indicating
  roughly linear behavior over this limited range rather than cubic growth.
- The true drivers are expression count, physical device count, model/block
  complexity, parameter count, and Jacobian nonzero structure, not state count
  alone.
- The increasing ratio shows the main VeraGrid scaling bottleneck: ANDES setup
  remains near 0.05--0.10 s, while VeraGrid compiles the complete symbolic DAE
  for the current network.

A broader preparation comparison includes case loading, because that is where
ANDES pays part of its numerical preparation:

6.4 Production small-signal calculation
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Case      ANDES [s]  VeraGrid [s]  VG/ANDES  Faster engine
--------  ---------  ------------  --------  -------------
Kundur     0.013852      0.030901    2.231x  ANDES 2.23x
IEEE14     0.002962      0.042896   14.480x  ANDES 14.48x
IEEE39     0.125438      0.055578    0.443x  VeraGrid 2.26x
NPCC140    0.342250      0.162901    0.476x  VeraGrid 2.10x
WECC179    0.376404      0.281134    0.747x  VeraGrid 1.34x

What happens in this phase:

- Build the state matrix or generalized matrix from the initialized DAE.
- Calculate every eigenvalue and the left/right eigenvectors.
- Calculate participation factors, damping ratios, and frequencies.
- Assemble the engine result object.

Interpretation and scalability:

- Small systems are dominated by fixed Python, SciPy, BLAS, allocation, and
  result-assembly overhead. The Kundur/IEEE14 factors are therefore poor
  predictors of large-system scaling.
- A crossover appears at IEEE39: VeraGrid becomes faster for all three larger
  cases. The numerical small-signal kernel is not VeraGrid's main bottleneck.
- Both engines calculate all modes with dense linear algebra. State/eigenvector
  storage is approximately O(n^2), while a full dense eigendecomposition is
  asymptotically O(n^3).
- The five systems are not scaled copies of one network. Algebraic dimension,
  sparsity, conditioning, model mix, BLAS kernels, and thread scheduling prevent
  a clean cubic trend from appearing in only five single samples.

9. CASE-BY-CASE INTERPRETATION
------------------------------

Kundur
~~~~~~

- ANDES spends 84.8% of its total loading/building the case.
- VeraGrid spends 92.4% of its total in the broad power-flow phase.
- VeraGrid RMS preparation is only 0.077 s and the modal matrices are too small
  for meaningful asymptotic conclusions.
- Kundur primarily compares fixed startup and preparation costs.

IEEE14
~~~~~~

- This is the largest total ratio: VeraGrid is 5.02x slower.
- VeraGrid power flow is 65.3% of its total, the combined RMS phase is 12.5%,
  and the additional audit-heavy export is 14.5%.
- The 56-state modal kernel is still dominated by fixed overhead.
- The export outlier must not be extrapolated to other cases.

IEEE39
~~~~~~

- VeraGrid power flow is 65.0% and combined RMS preparation is 29.6% of its
  total.
- VeraGrid production small signal is already 2.26x faster than ANDES.
- This case clearly demonstrates that VeraGrid's performance gap occurs before
  modal analysis rather than in the eigensolution itself.

NPCC140
~~~~~~~

- VeraGrid power flow and combined RMS preparation are 40.6% and 43.0% of its
  total respectively; together they account for 83.6%.
- VeraGrid production small signal is 2.10x faster and export is 2.40x faster.
- These gains reduce the complete workflow disadvantage to 2.05x, the smallest
  total ratio among the five cases.

WECC179
~~~~~~~

- VeraGrid power flow is 32.4%, combined RMS preparation is 42.0%, production
  small signal is 4.5%, and export is 11.7% of its total.
- VeraGrid production small signal is 1.34x faster.
- Export is nearly equal because both engines serialize very large matrices.
- The total 3.21x difference is primarily a preparation/compilation difference,
  not a modal-analysis difference.

10. TECHNICAL CONCLUSIONS
-------------------------

1. Numerical validation is successful.
   Operating points, state matrices, every eigenvalue, and participation factors
   agree within very small numerical errors in all five systems. No material
   physical-model mismatch remains in the final benchmark results.

2. ANDES is faster in the current complete workflow.
   The measured end-to-end advantage ranges from 2.05x to 5.02x. This is the
   correct conclusion when network creation, power flow, dynamic preparation,
   small signal, and result export are all included.

3. The complete-workflow advantage is not an eigensolver advantage.
   VeraGrid is faster in production small signal for IEEE39, NPCC140, and
   WECC179 and faster in every isolated eigensolution/PF warm repeat. The main
   total-time difference occurs before modal analysis.

4. VeraGrid's first visible bottleneck is the broad power-flow phase.
   PowerFlowDriver.run() includes numerical-circuit compilation and result
   processing in addition to Newton. Its nearly constant two-second timing
   suggests a large fixed/cold cost. A future pure solver benchmark must split
   circuit compilation, Ybus/index construction, Newton iterations, and result
   assembly.

5. VeraGrid's main size-dependent bottleneck is RMS DAE preparation.
   Building, explicitly initializing, and compiling the system-specific symbolic
   DAE rises from 0.077 s to 2.599 s. The observed growth is reasonable over the
   tested range, but ANDES avoids much of this runtime work through pregenerated
   numerical model calls and cheaper system setup.

6. State-matrix construction has optimization potential.
   VeraGrid's warm matrix diagnostic is approximately 3.25--4.86x slower, partly
   because it reevaluates Jacobians and performs additional matrix conversion.
   This ratio is a profiling clue rather than a like-for-like kernel comparison.

7. VeraGrid's modal linear algebra is already competitive.
   The large-case production and diagnostic results show that eigenvalues,
   left/right eigenvectors, and participation factors are not the limiting part
   of the current VeraGrid workflow.

8. Export must be kept separate from numerical performance.
   Dense matrix CSV output scales approximately as O(n^2) and materially affects
   large-case totals. IEEE14 also writes additional VeraGrid audit artifacts,
   making its export phase intentionally broader than the common minimum.

9. The measurements indicate scaling behavior but do not prove asymptotic laws.
   The cases differ in topology, algebraic dimension, device mix, source format,
   conditioning, and model complexity. They are not scaled copies of one base
   network. State count alone cannot explain every timing difference.

10. A definitive performance study requires repeated controlled runs.
    Each case should be executed multiple times in fresh processes, separating
    cold and warm runs and reporting median, dispersion, BLAS/thread settings,
    power-flow iteration counts, and filesystem-cache conditions. The current
    measurements are valid for numerical validation and bottleneck discovery,
    but not for claiming statistically precise speed factors.

Overall conclusion:

    ANDES finishes the current normal workflow faster because it reaches modal
    analysis with less runtime preparation. Once the numerical RMS problem is
    ready, VeraGrid is competitive and is faster in the medium and large
    production small-signal calculations. Performance work should therefore
    focus first on power-flow preparation and reusable/cached RMS DAE
    compilation, not on replacing the modal eigensolver.

11. REPRODUCIBLE ARTIFACTS
--------------------------

Each engine result folder contains a timing CSV and JSON with the fields phase,
seconds, relation_to_normal_workflow_total, scope, state_count, and mode_count.
Each comparison folder contains timing_comparison.csv, the visual timing table,
the numerical comparison CSVs/plots, and comparison_report.txt.

The relation and scope columns are the machine-readable authority for deciding
whether a value is included in the normal total. The benchmark scripts can be
run directly from PyCharm; the ANDES and comparison preset enums select Kundur,
IEEE14, IEEE39, NPCC140, or WECC179 without requiring a terminal command.
